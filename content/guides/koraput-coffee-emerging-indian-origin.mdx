## Geography

Koraput district sits in the southern **Eastern Ghats** of Odisha, bordering Andhra Pradesh. The coffee-growing pockets — Pottangi, Semiliguda, Dasmantpur, Lamtaput — lie on plateau and hill slopes between roughly 900 and 1,200 m, with a few lots higher. The Eastern Ghats are older, more eroded and lower than the Western Ghats, with a shorter, sharper monsoon.

## The farms

Koraput coffee is overwhelmingly **smallholder and tribal-grown**, often on land under a acre or two, frequently intercropped with pepper, banana and forest shade trees. Development agencies and farmer foundations have organised much of the recent planting and wet-milling infrastructure. This is closer to the East African smallholder model than to the Indian estate model.

## Varieties

The workhorse is **S795**, a rust-tolerant Indian selection with a balanced, nutty profile. **Chandragiri** brings more sweetness and body. **HSD (Hibrido de Timor)** lots give a cleaner, more citric, more "washed-Ethiopia" character and are increasingly kept separate as specialty micro-lots.

## Processing

Because the milling is new, Koraput has skipped straight to processing diversity: fully washed, natural (whole cherry dried on raised beds for 25–30 days), honey/pulped-natural, and small anaerobic-fermentation experiments. Washed lots are clean with cocoa, brown sugar and mild citrus; naturals push dark cherry and chocolate.

## Why it matters

Koraput gives Indian coffee something it was short on: a young, traceable, smallholder origin with real processing variety and lots still cheap enough to experiment with. That is exactly the profile buyers look for before an origin gets "discovered".
