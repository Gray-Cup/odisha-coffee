## Washed

The cherry is pulped, the remaining mucilage is removed by fermentation and washing, and the parchment bean is dried. Nothing fruity is left touching the bean.

- **Cup:** clean, transparent, higher perceived acidity, origin character front and centre.
- **Consistency:** high, fewer surprises bag to bag.
- **Roasting:** predictable; even moisture, even colour development.
- **Best for:** single-origin filter, pour-over, lighter roasts, anywhere you want clarity.

## Natural

The whole cherry is dried intact for weeks; the bean ferments slowly inside the fruit before hulling.

- **Cup:** heavy body, dark fruit, wine/berry, chocolate, lower acidity, sometimes boozy.
- **Consistency:** lower, drying is harder to control, more cup variation and occasional ferment defects.
- **Roasting:** trickier; uneven density and sugar content, easy to scorch, needs a gentler, longer development.
- **Best for:** espresso blends, milk drinks, darker profiles, adding body to a blend.

## Honey (in between)

Pulped but dried with some mucilage on, sweeter and rounder than washed, cleaner and more consistent than natural.

## The roaster's answer

Buy **washed** if you sell single-origin filter and want repeatability. Buy **natural** for body and fruit in espresso blends, and price in more QC time. Most roasters carry both. Honey is the low-risk way to add sweetness.
