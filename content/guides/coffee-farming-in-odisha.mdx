## Where and how high

Koraput coffee grows on the plateaus and hill slopes of the southern **Eastern Ghats**, mostly between **900 and 1,200 m**. Lower than the Western Ghats estates, with thinner soils and a shorter monsoon.

## Climate

A single south-west monsoon (June–September), a cooler dry winter, and pre-monsoon "blossom showers" in March–April that trigger flowering. Irrigation is limited, so timely rain matters.

## Shade and intercropping

Coffee is grown under a canopy of silver oak and native forest trees, often intercropped with **black pepper, banana, and turmeric**, which spreads the smallholder's income and risk across crops.

## Varieties

Mostly **Arabica**, S795, Chandragiri, HSD, some Cauvery and small SLN 9 plots. Robusta is minor here compared to South India.

## The season

- **Mar–Apr**: flowering after blossom showers.
- **Jun–Oct**: cherry development through the monsoon.
- **Nov–Feb**: ripening and hand-harvest of red cherries.

## Processing and drying

Cherries go to village or estate wet mills for washed, natural or honey processing, then dry on **raised beds** to 10–12% moisture over 2–5 weeks before resting and hulling.

## Who farms it

Overwhelmingly **tribal smallholders**, often on plots under two acres, organised through farmer foundations and development agencies that built much of the recent milling infrastructure.
