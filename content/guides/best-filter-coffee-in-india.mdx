Traditional South Indian filter coffee is a **method and a blend**, not a single bean. Hot water drips slowly through a metal filter to make a strong decoction, which is then cut with hot milk and sugar and pulled between tumbler and davara for froth.

## The ratio decides the cup

Filter blends are described by Arabica:Robusta ratio:

- **80:20 / 90:10** — mostly Arabica: aromatic, smoother, mild bitterness.
- **70:30** — the classic "kaapi" balance: strong, full crema, more kick.
- **With chicory** (often 70:30 coffee:chicory) — adds body, a woody sweetness, stretches the coffee, and is the "authentic" South Indian taste for many people.

## Chicory or no chicory

Chicory is a roasted root, not coffee. It has no caffeine, adds a dark syrupy body, and is traditional in Tamil Nadu and Karnataka filter coffee. If you want 100% coffee, look for "pure Arabica" or "no chicory" on the label.

## Roast and grind

Filter coffee wants a **medium to medium-dark roast** and a **fine-ish grind** — finer than pour-over, coarser than espresso. Buy powder fresh and in small quantities; ground filter coffee stales fast.
