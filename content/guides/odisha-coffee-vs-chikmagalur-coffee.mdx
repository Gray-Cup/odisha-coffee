Chikmagalur is where Indian coffee began, legend has Baba Budan smuggling seven beans from Yemen to the hills above the town in the 1600s. Koraput, Odisha is a 21st-century origin. Comparing them shows why "Indian coffee" isn't a single flavour.

## Chikmagalur

- Western Ghats, ~1,000–1,500 m, some of India's highest coffee.
- Long history, established estates, both Arabica and Robusta.
- Cup: balanced, medium-full body, chocolate, mild spice, gentle acidity.

## Koraput

- Eastern Ghats, ~900–1,200 m, almost all Arabica.
- New origin, smallholder, more processing variety (washed, natural, honey, anaerobic).
- Cup: clean and cocoa-ish (washed), fruit-forward (natural), more lot-to-lot variation.

## Not one category

High-grown Chikmagalur Arabica, low-grown Coorg Robusta, mellow Northeast lots and bright Koraput washed coffees are all "Indian coffee" and taste nothing alike. Treat the label as a starting point, not a flavour.
