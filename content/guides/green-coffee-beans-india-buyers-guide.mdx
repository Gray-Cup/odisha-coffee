Green coffee is the raw, unroasted seed of the coffee cherry, dried to a stable moisture level and sorted for size and defects. It is what roasters buy. This guide covers how to read an Indian green coffee lot before you commit.

## What you're evaluating

**Grade** — In India, washed Arabica is graded (best to commodity) roughly Plantation AAA / AA / A / PB (peaberry) / B / BBB, based mostly on screen size and defect count. Grade is a *sorting* spec, not a *taste* score — a well-farmed A can out-cup a lazy AAA — but higher grades give bigger, more uniform beans that roast more evenly.

**Screen size** — Bean size in 1/64 inch. Screen 17+ is large and uniform; screen 14–15 roasts faster and less evenly. Uniformity matters more than raw size.

**Moisture** — Should sit at **10–12%**. Below 9% the beans are brittle and bake; above 12.5% they mould and lose sweetness in storage.

**Processing** — Washed (clean, transparent), natural (fruit-forward, heavier), honey (sweet middle), anaerobic (funky, limited). This drives cup character more than grade.

**Density & crop year** — Denser (higher-grown) beans take more heat. Current-crop beans roast predictably; past-crop beans go flat and papery.

## How to actually buy

1. **Get a sample.** Roast 100–250 g, cup it, and check it roasts evenly before ordering volume.
2. **Confirm moisture and crop year** in writing.
3. **Start with one washed and one natural** to learn the origin.
4. **Buy what you'll use in ~6 months** — green coffee stales too.
