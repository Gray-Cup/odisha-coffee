## What "best" means for Indian coffee specifically

India grows coffee in three broad belts — the southern hills of Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu; the Northeast (Assam, Arunachal, Nagaland); and the Eastern Ghats of **Koraput, Odisha**. Most large Indian coffee brands blend across these origins for consistency. Smaller specialty roasters do the opposite: they keep lots separate so you can taste the difference between a washed Koraput Arabica and a natural-process Chikmagalur.

Both approaches are valid. A traditional South Indian filter blend is *supposed* to be a blend. A single-origin pour-over is *supposed* to be one farm. The mistake is treating one as inherently better than the other.

## How to actually choose

1. **Check the roast date.** No date on the bag is a red flag. Aim to drink within 4–6 weeks of roast.
2. **Match roast level to brew.** Light roasts suit pour-over and filter; medium-dark suits espresso and milk drinks.
3. **Buy small first.** A 250 g bag tells you everything you need to know before you commit to a kilo.
4. **Try one washed and one natural** from the same region — it is the fastest way to learn what you like.

Start with a single-origin Koraput washed coffee and a natural from the same district, brew them side by side, and you will know more about your own taste than any list can tell you.
