Coffee grading is a **sorting system**, not a quality verdict. It sorts green beans by physical characteristics so a buyer knows what a bag will look and roast like. It does not tell you how the coffee tastes.

## Indian washed Arabica grades

| Grade | Screen size | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **AAA / Plantation A** | 17+ | Largest, most uniform beans, low defects. Roasts most evenly. |
| **AA** | 16+ | Large, uniform, low defects. The specialty workhorse grade. |
| **A** | 15+ | Solid commercial-to-specialty; slightly less uniform. |
| **PB (Peaberry)** | round bean | A single round seed instead of two flat halves (~5% of a crop). Often denser; roasts a little differently. |
| **B+ / B** | 14+ / 13+ | Smaller, more size variation. Blend and commodity use. |
| **BBB** | 12+ | Lowest washed grade. Instant coffee, low-cost blends. |

Naturals, honey and HSD lots use their own labels (often "AA Naturals", "AA HSD Specialty") because the sorting is done on the finished natural/honey bean.

## What grade does tell you

- **Roast evenness**: uniform size = uniform heat transfer = fewer scorched and underdeveloped beans in the same batch.
- **Screen consistency** for your roast curves.
- **Defect ceiling**: higher grades cap primary/secondary defects.

## What grade does NOT tell you

- **Cup quality.** Terroir, farming, picking ripeness, fermentation and drying decide flavour. A carefully farmed A beats a careless AAA.
- **Processing character.** Grade is size; process is taste.
- **Freshness.** A AAA lot from two crops ago is still stale.
