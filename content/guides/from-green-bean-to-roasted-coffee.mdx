## 1. Farm and harvest

Smallholders in Koraput pick ripe red cherries by hand, usually November to February. Ripeness at picking sets the ceiling on cup quality — under-ripe cherries taste green and astringent no matter what happens next.

## 2. Processing

At the mill the cherries are sorted and processed:
- **Washed** — pulped, fermented, washed, dried on parchment.
- **Natural** — dried whole on raised beds for 25–30 days.
- **Honey** — pulped, dried with mucilage still on.

## 3. Drying and resting

Beans dry to **10–12% moisture**, then rest in parchment for weeks to stabilise. Hulling removes the parchment layer to reveal the **green bean**.

## 4. Grading and green sale

Green beans are sorted by screen size and defects (AAA, AA, A, PB…) and sold to roasters:

## 5. Roasting

The roaster applies heat over 8–14 minutes. Sugars caramelise, acids break down, moisture drops, the bean expands and cracks (first crack ~196°C, second crack ~224°C). Where the roaster stops — light, medium, dark — decides acidity vs body vs bitterness.

## 6. Cooling, resting, packing

Roasted coffee is crash-cooled, rested 24–72 hours to degas CO₂, then packed in valve bags with a roast date.

## 7. Brew

Ground fresh, brewed as filter, pour-over, French press or espresso.
